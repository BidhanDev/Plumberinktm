How this business started

Suresh Maharjan went onto his first building site in Kirtipur at seventeen, carrying lengths of GI pipe for an uncle who did not believe in explaining anything twice. He spent eleven years on new construction, which is where you learn what a wall looks like on the inside and why the cheap joint always fails in the same place.

By 2014 he was tired of watching good work get buried under bad work, so he and Sunita registered the business from their own flat. The first year was tap washers and midnight calls from neighbours. It grew because of a rule they set on day one and have not broken since: tell the customer the price before touching anything, and if the job turns out bigger, stop and say so.

That sounds small. In a trade where most quotes arrive after the wall is already open, it turns out to be the whole business.

Four things we will not do

Easier to judge a plumber by what they refuse than by what they promise.

1

Pour acid down a drain

It clears the symptom for a month and eats the pipe for good. We rod it or machine it instead.

2

Open a wall before quoting

Once the wall is open your bargaining position is gone. We price first, then open.

3

Fit a geyser without a safety valve

We have been asked to skip it to save money. The answer is no, every time.

4

Run uPVC on a hot line

It sags, it leaks, and the person who finds out is whoever owns the house in five years.
