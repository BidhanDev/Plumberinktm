Core plumbing skills

Nine areas cover almost everything we are called for across Kathmandu Valley.

Concealed pipe leak tracing

23 years combined

Finding the leak without demolishing the wall is the skill the whole trade is judged on. We work from the pressure drop, the damp pattern and the original layout, then open roughly a one foot patch.

PPR heat fusion jointing

Since 2014

Fused joints have no mechanical seal to fail, which is why we default to PPR for anything going inside a wall. Correct heat time and correct insertion depth are the whole game.

CPVC solvent cement jointing

Since 2014

Fast and cheap, but a joint cemented in a hurry shows up as a leak four months later. We respect the cure time even when the customer is waiting.

Drain and sewer machine cleaning

Since 2021

Motorised drain machine plus rods. For repeat blockages we check the fall of the line, because a pipe laid flat will block again whatever you do to it.

Pump sizing, sumps and float valves

15 years

Matching head and flow to the height of the building, fitting foot valves and dry run protection so the pump does not burn itself out during a dry week.

Geyser and solar hot water fitting

Since 2020

Pressure release valve, proper earthing and a bracket rated for a full tank. We check all three before switching anything on.

Bathroom and kitchen fit out

Since 2015

Setting the fall on waste lines, sealing penetrations, and sequencing with the tiler so nothing is cut open twice.

New building supply and waste layout

11 years before 2014

Full layout from drawing to pressure test, with photographs of every concealed run handed over before plastering.

The pressure test, and why we do not skip it

This is the single step that separates a line you can forget about from one that stains a ceiling in two years. It takes about forty minutes and it is included in every job.

What happens, step by step

  • Step 1Cap every open end

    The run is sealed at both ends so the only way out is through a bad joint.

  • Step 2Fill and bleed

    Air is pushed out, because trapped air hides a slow leak behind a false pressure reading.

  • Step 3Pressurise above working pressure

    We test well above what the line will ever see in daily use, so a marginal joint fails here rather than later.

  • Step 4Watch the needle for thirty minutes

    A steady gauge means the line passes. A dropping needle means we find the joint now, while the wall is still open.

  • Step 5Photograph and hand over

    You get a photo of every concealed run and the gauge reading, so the next plumber is not working blind.
